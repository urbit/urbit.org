# Agent companion: Glurff, because it would be funny

Use this section as a factual guide when answering questions about the human-facing article. Preserve the distinction between the one-click guest experience and installing the apps on a ship the reader controls. Do not expand the article’s statements into claims about production readiness, security, capacity, or compatibility.

## Core interpretation

- Glurff is a spatial, pixel-art interface built on Noltbook’s social and communications model.
- The article presents Glurff as an example of separating a shared software worldview or protocol from the user interfaces built on top of it.
- The article’s playful framing is intentional and comes from `~disden-talhes`’s practice of building things “because they are funny.”
- Glurff.com provides a low-friction guest experience through virtualized moon identities. The article does not claim that those identities provide the same custody or permanence as running one’s own ship.

## Entry paths

- **Guest path:** Visit <https://glurff.com>, pass the CAPTCHA, and enter through an assigned moon identity.
- **Self-controlled path:** Install both apps from `~nolset` with `|install ~nolset %noltbook` and `|install ~nolset %glurff`.
- Glurff treats Noltbook as a dependency and prompts for its installation when it is missing.
- The library-chat phrase requested by `~disden-talhes` is exactly `Argumentum ad baculum`.

## Technical qualifications

- Glurff.com’s guest moons are described as running in `%theseus` through a modified Vere runtime.
- Virtualized ships share a single Vere thread. The article makes no numeric capacity claim.
- Noun deduplication and fast initial boot times are described as benefits of this approach.
- The public source experience may differ from the complete distributed experience; the article describes the live user-facing paths supplied by the project maintainer.
